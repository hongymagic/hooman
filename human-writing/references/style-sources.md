# Writing craft from published examples

Optional reading for maintaining the skill or understanding its editorial choices.
These observations concern phrasing, rhythm and voice. They are our interpretations
of selected posts, not official style policies or claims about who wrote them.
Accessed **22 September 2026**. Publication dates below describe the articles;
historical technical claims were not verified as current advice.

## Techniques worth borrowing

**Name the thing and say what it does.** OpenAI and Netflix explanations often make
an abstract subject readable by naming an action, the thing affected and the result.
Prefer that relationship to praise about how powerful or sophisticated something is.
Keep the precise term when it carries meaning; explain it nearby when readers need
help. Simpler language does not require vaguer language.

**Let the next sentence answer the reader's next concern.** The useful transitions
in these posts follow a question, consequence or limitation already raised. A short
question can create a natural turn when its answer matters. Announcing another
section, adding a ceremonial summary or manufacturing suspense does not provide
that connection by itself.

**Use contrast to explain a difference.** Anthropic's definitions and Netflix's
comparisons give the alternatives substance. Keep a contrast when it clarifies a
choice or corrects a likely misunderstanding. Remove a slogan that merely restates
the same claim with more emphasis.

**Put confidence in the evidence.** Concrete observations sound more credible than
claims of importance. Limitations can be expressed plainly, next to the finding
they qualify. Preserve that certainty level while cutting duplicated hedges; do not
replace a cautious but accurate sentence with an unsupported assurance.

**Keep the writer's cadence.** Holman's posts illustrate how questions, asides and
short sentences can carry an established voice. Their effectiveness depends on
context, not a punctuation formula. Preserve such choices in a supplied sample;
do not manufacture the author's humour, opinions or experience. Formal technical
prose has a voice too.

## Posts inspected

### OpenAI

- [Scaling PostgreSQL to power 800 million ChatGPT users](https://openai.com/index/scaling-postgresql/)
  — 22 January 2026; main narrative and sections through the conclusion. Concrete
  subjects and verbs make chains of events legible. Explanations attach the
  consequence to the action that produces it. Borrow that specificity, not the
  promotional framing or the article's scale claims.
- [Unrolling the Codex agent loop](https://openai.com/index/unrolling-the-codex-agent-loop/)
  — 23 January 2026; selected introduction, terminology, loop explanation,
  performance discussion and conclusion, not every code example. It establishes
  what an overloaded name means before using it and adds detail in manageable
  stages. Meaningful questions orient the reader rather than merely punctuate prose.

### Anthropic

- [Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)
  — 19 December 2024; main body through the summary, excluding a full appendix
  review. Definitions make a distinction useful; parallel passages help readers
  compare related ideas. Repeated structure can therefore aid understanding. Its
  slogans and closing reframing are not models the skill must reproduce.
- [A postmortem of three recent issues](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/a-postmortem-of-three-recent-issues)
  — 17 September 2025; main body. Direct descriptions of symptoms, findings and
  changes do more work than generic reassurance. Wording distinguishes what was
  established from what remained uncertain without making every sentence tentative.

### Netflix TechBlog

- [Rethinking Netflix's Edge Load Balancing](https://netflixtechblog.com/netflix-edge-load-balancing-695308b5548c)
  — 28 September 2018; complete accessible body and appendix. Concrete examples
  precede dense explanation, and consequences stay close to their causes. Keep
  evidence qualifications in the prose; the long article structure is optional.
- [Engineering Trade-Offs and The Netflix API Re-Architecture](https://netflixtechblog.com/engineering-trade-offs-and-the-netflix-api-re-architecture-64f122b277dd)
  — 23 August 2016; complete accessible body. A familiar user action makes the
  opening understandable. Both alternatives receive a fair description, and
  conditional wording keeps an experiment distinct from a settled decision.
- [The Evolution of Cassandra Data Movement at Netflix](https://netflixtechblog.com/the-evolution-of-cassandra-data-movement-at-netflix-6e13329c80a1)
  — 18 May 2026; complete accessible body. Describing what changed gives the
  account substance. Broad transformation language adds less; tightening must
  preserve limitations that sit beside the success claims.

### Zach Holman

- [UTC is Enough for Everyone, Right?](https://zachholman.com/talk/utc-is-enough-for-everyone-right)
  — May 2018; complete article, including its 23 September 2021 update. The
  [talks index](https://zachholman.com/talks) lists 29 May 2018. A concrete complication
  gives the next explanation a reason to exist. Questions and asides vary the pace;
  the author's jokes, profanity and comic dialogue are not default writing tools.
- [How GitHub Writes Blog Posts](https://zachholman.com/posts/how-github-writes-blog-posts/)
  — 19 January 2015; publishing, continuous integration, collaboration and editing
  sections. A familiar analogy introduces an unfamiliar process. Its local checks
  on habitual openings support cutting empty preambles, not universal phrase bans.
- [Three Quotes from Early GitHubbers](https://zachholman.com/posts/three-quotes-from-early-githubbers)
  — 11 April 2017; writing section. Protect the main point from competing detail.
  A fixed cutting quota would lose that judgement; three-part organisation can work.
- [The Apple Voice](https://zachholman.com/posts/the-apple-voice/)
  — 2 February 2012; sections about selecting writing standards. Match samples to
  the document: an announcement and a formal explanation need not share a register.

## Boundaries

No article passages, code, images or distinctive anecdotes are bundled here. The
skill's examples are original. These cited articles retain their respective rights;
the package's MIT licence does not relicense them. No open reuse licence was
established for this material. Holman licence checks covered rendered pages and
links; direct HTML access was blocked, so markup-level checking was incomplete.

These posts contain effective writing alongside habits we would sometimes edit.
Borrow the useful choice in context, not a whole author's persona or a mandatory
article outline. No phrase, sentence shape or stylistic preference establishes AI
authorship. The aim is clearer, more individual writing with the meaning intact.
