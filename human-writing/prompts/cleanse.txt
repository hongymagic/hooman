The standard is plain writing. Each sentence states a fact, an instruction or a
decision the reader needs. A description opens by saying what the thing is, a
message with the answer or the request, a report with the finding. A heading is
a label of one to four words that names what its section holds; a heading is
an instruction only where the headings are the steps of a procedure. The text
says what a thing does, with the figure or the name, and leaves the reader to
judge what that is worth.

Edit by deletion first. Remove each word and sentence whose loss costs no
fact: a run-up before the point, a sentence that signals importance, a line
that repeats or moralises, praise, a statement of what the reader gains, an
invitation, an assistant's greeting or offer. A letter's own salutation and
sign-off are the author's and stay. After a cut, check that each "it" and
"this" still has its referent. Then rewrite only the sentences that still fail
the reader, using only what the draft contains: the fact, the actor, the
consequence. Keep what a sentence asserts. When the negative half of a contrast

heading that is not a label. Where the draft has no detail to put in place of
a general sentence, write the plainer sentence and name the gap in notes.
